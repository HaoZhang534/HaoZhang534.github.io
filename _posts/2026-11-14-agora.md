A research agent can run an experiment and write down what happened. With many agents, the harder problem is deciding which record to read and which previous finding to trust.

## Contributions become a graph

[Agora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18094) stores results, hypotheses, insights, and verification reports as an append-only Git-backed directed acyclic graph. Parent links preserve provenance. Searchable views expose promising findings, neglected directions, and verification status; diversity-aware recommendations guide attention beyond the current leader.

In the reported weight-transfer study, 13 workers contribute over nearly 12 days. They publish 1,703 contributions and improve a frozen 119.6M-parameter target from 3.39 to 1.899 bits per byte without target training data or gradient updates. These figures describe the particular transfer task and evaluator, not a general scientific-discovery benchmark.

## Memory needs structure

Consider two hypothetical entries: “method B scored well” and “method B reproduces entry A, changes its initialization, and was verified under configuration C.” The second is more useful because it separates a claim from its supporting execution and states what changed.

A flat conversation log can preserve the same words but make these relationships expensive to recover. A graph makes dependency explicit. It can also preserve unsuccessful branches, which may keep later workers from repeating an already understood failure.

## What the study does and does not show

The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18094v4#S4.SS7) records human interventions, including changes to diversity views and a worker prompt during the run. Most of the observed score reduction arrives early. Measuring the causal benefit of shared memory or diversity guidance therefore requires a matched comparison, rather than attributing every later improvement to one interface feature.

## The broader design question

More memory is not necessarily better retrieval. A useful research workspace must help agents locate credible evidence, inspect the experiment behind it, and decide whether further exploration is worthwhile. That is an information-allocation problem as much as a storage problem.

This interpretation connects Agora to [OSWorld-Pro](/blog/osworld-pro/): both make intermediate work inspectable, although one records research contributions and the other evaluates actions in a desktop trajectory.
